Notes — Author, Theme & Summary

Concept Notes, Section-wise

(a) About the Author — R.C. Hutchinson

The essay "The Pace for Living" is written by R.C. Hutchinson, an English writer known for his warm, reflective and gently humorous style of prose. In this essay, he uses a simple personal experience — watching a play in Dublin — as a starting point to comment on the ever-increasing speed of modern life and how it affects ordinary people, including the writer himself.

हिंदी में सार: यह निबंध आर.सी. हचिन्सन (R.C. Hutchinson) द्वारा लिखा गया है, जो अपनी सहज, चिंतनशील तथा हल्के-फुल्के हास्य-भरे लेखन के लिए जाने जाते हैं।

(b) Central Theme of the Essay

The central theme of "The Pace for Living" is the ever-increasing speed of modern life and the strain it places on human beings. Through the figure of an anxious, weak-hearted corn-merchant who is alarmed even by the thought of an aeroplane flying at 1,000 miles an hour, the writer captures the fear and fatigue that rapid technological progress can cause. At the same time, the writer — who calls himself a "slow thinker" — shows through his own experience of struggling to follow a fast-moving film that not everyone is built for this frantic pace. The essay does not condemn speed and progress altogether; instead, it pleads for balance — for a pace of living that leaves room for reflection, patience and genuine human relationships, rather than treating life as a race to be run as fast as possible.

हिंदी में सार: इस निबंध का मुख्य विषय आधुनिक जीवन की बढ़ती हुई तेज़ रफ़्तार और उससे मनुष्य पर पड़ने वाला दबाव है। लेखक तेज़ी का पूर्ण विरोध नहीं करते, बल्कि गति और चिंतन के बीच संतुलन बनाए रखने की सलाह देते हैं।

(c) Summary of the Essay

The essay opens with the writer recalling a play he once watched in Dublin. Its chief character was an ageing corn-merchant from a small Irish country town — a man burdened with anxieties: his heart was weak ("dicky"), he suspected his nephew of cheating him in business, and he was upset that his wife had the notion of spending ten pounds on a holiday. In a moment of despair, he cries out about a new aeroplane that can fly at 1,000 miles an hour, exclaiming that such speed is simply "too fast." His outburst is not really about aeroplanes at all — it is the cry of a man who feels that the whole pace of modern life has outrun him.

The writer then turns to his own life to show that he understands this feeling very well. He confesses that he is a "slow thinker" — someone who often works out the right answer to a question only after the moment for using it has passed. He recalls how, at the cinema, he frequently loses track of a fast-moving plot and has to lean over to his wife to ask who a particular character is, long after the rest of the audience has already understood. Such slow thinkers, he says, are genuinely handicapped in a world that rewards quick reflexes, sharp presence of mind and instant answers — whether in an interview, an examination, or an everyday conversation.

Yet the writer is careful to clarify that he does not dislike speed or rapid movement in every field. Progress, machines and fast transport have their own value and place in life. What he objects to is the growing assumption that faster is always better — that a well-lived life must be a hurried one. He suggests, gently and with humour, that there is room in the world for both the quick and the slow, and that true living requires not just speed but also the patience to notice, to feel, and to reflect.

हिंदी में सार: लेखक डबलिन में देखे गए एक नाटक का ज़िक्र करते हैं जिसमें एक कमज़ोर हृदय वाला वृद्ध अनाज-व्यापारी 1000 मील प्रति घंटे की रफ़्तार वाले हवाई जहाज़ की बात सुनकर घबरा जाता है। लेखक स्वयं को भी "धीमा सोचने वाला" (Slow Thinker) बताते हैं और यह दर्शाते हैं कि तेज़ रफ़्तार दुनिया में धीरे सोचने वाले लोग भी नुकसान में रहते हैं, फिर भी हर क्षेत्र में तेज़ी बुरी नहीं होती।

(d) Key Phrases and Quotations to Remember

  • "Now that's too fast!" — the corn-merchant's cry on hearing of an aeroplane doing 1,000 miles an hour; it sums up his fear of being left behind by modern progress.
  • "A man of many anxieties" — describes the corn-merchant, whose worries about his health, his nephew and his wife's holiday plan are made worse by the pressure of a fast-changing world.
  • "Slow thinker" — how the writer describes himself; a person who needs more time to process information and respond, and is therefore at a disadvantage in a hurried world.
  • The cinema episode — the writer's confession that he loses track of a film's story and must ask his wife for help; used as a light, self-deprecating illustration of being a slow thinker.

हिंदी में सार: "अब यह बहुत तेज़ है!" — अनाज-व्यापारी का यह वाक्य आधुनिक प्रगति से पीछे छूट जाने के डर को दर्शाता है, जबकि सिनेमा वाली घटना लेखक के "धीमे सोचने" को हास्यपूर्ण ढंग से स्पष्ट करती है।

(e) Message for Students

The essay reminds us that not everyone thinks or reacts at the same speed, and that this is perfectly normal. Being a "slow thinker" is not a fault — it is simply a different way of engaging with the world, and it often comes with its own strengths, such as patience and careful reflection. The message for students is to resist the pressure to rush through everything and to remember that a meaningful life is measured not by how fast we move through it, but by how thoughtfully and fully we live each part of it.

हिंदी में सार: हर व्यक्ति की सोचने और प्रतिक्रिया देने की गति अलग होती है, और यह पूरी तरह सामान्य है। जीवन की सार्थकता उसकी गति में नहीं, बल्कि हम उसे कितनी सोच-समझ और पूर्णता के साथ जीते हैं, उसमें छिपी है।
